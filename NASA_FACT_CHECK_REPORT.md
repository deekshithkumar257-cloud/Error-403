## 2. Artifact-by-Artifact Detailed Verification

### 2.1 Apollo 11 Lunar Module Descent Stage
* **Official Registry:** NASA NSSDCA ID: `1969-059C` | Callsign: *Eagle (LM-5)*
* **Launch Date:** July 16, 1969, 13:32:00 UTC (Saturn V SA-506, Kennedy Space Center LC-39A).
* **Lunar Landing Date:** July 20, 1969, 20:17:40 UTC.
* **Surface Duration:** 21 hours, 36 minutes, 20 seconds.
* **Historical Location:** Mare Tranquillitatis (*Sea of Tranquility*), **0.67408° N, 23.47297° E** (verified by Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera - LROC).
* **Hardware Location Type:** Permanent Lunar Surface Preserved Artifact.
* **Original Purpose:** Controlled descent from lunar orbit to the lunar surface using the throttleable Descent Propulsion System (DPS); served as the landing gear, equipment stowage bay (MESA & EASEP), and stationary launch pad for the Apollo 11 Ascent Stage.
* **Key Equipment & Instruments:**
  * *Descent Propulsion System (DPS):* 4,400 N to 43,900 N throttleable hypergolic rocket engine (Aerozine-50 and Nitrogen Tetroxide).
  * *Landing Gear:* 4 cantilever legs with crushable aluminum honeycomb cartridges and 37-inch lunar contact sensing probe wires.
  * *Laser Ranging Retroreflector (LRRR):* 100-prism silica optical array.
  * *Passive Seismic Experiment Package (PSEP):* Solar-powered lunar seismometer (operated 21 days).
  * *Modular Equipment Stowage Assembly (MESA):* Westinghouse B&W lunar TV camera, sample bags, lunar tools.
* **Major Scientific Discoveries & Achievements:**
  * Returned 21.55 kg (47.5 lbs) of lunar regolith and basalt rocks, dating Mare Tranquillitatis to ~3.7 billion years old.
  * The LRRR established that the Moon is spiraling away from Earth at 3.8 cm (1.5 inches) per year due to tidal friction and provided high-precision validation of Einstein's General Relativity Equivalence Principle.
* **Operational Status:**
  * Batteries depleted July 21, 1969.
  * **Status:** Inactive. The passive LRRR retroreflector array requires no electricity and continues to be pinged with lasers by Earth observatories today.

### 2.2 Apollo Lunar Roving Vehicles (LRV)
* **Official Registry:** NASA NSSDCA IDs: `1971-063D` (LRV-1), `1972-031D` (LRV-2), `1972-096D` (LRV-3).
* **Missions & Crewed Landings:**
  * **Apollo 15 (LRV-1):** Launched July 26, 1971; Landed July 30, 1971 at Hadley-Apennine (26.1322° N, 3.6339° E). Traverse: 27.8 km.
  * **Apollo 16 (LRV-2):** Launched April 16, 1972; Landed April 21, 1972 at Descartes Highlands (8.9730° S, 15.5002° E). Traverse: 26.6 km.
  * **Apollo 17 (LRV-3):** Launched December 7, 1972; Landed December 11, 1972 at Taurus-Littrow (20.1908° N, 30.7717° E). Traverse: 35.9 km.
* **Hardware Location Type:** Permanent Lunar Surface Preserved Artifacts (exactly 3 rovers remain on the Moon).
* **Original Purpose:** Expand astronaut exploration traverse radius from hundreds of meters to tens of kilometers, transport heavy geological field tools and samples (up to 490 kg payload), and provide mobile color television broadcasting.
* **Key Equipment & Instruments:**
  * Four independent 0.25-hp DC electric series-wound wheel motors with 80:1 harmonic drive transmissions.
  * Woven zinc-coated steel wire-mesh tires with titanium chevron traction treads (rubber tires would fail in vacuum and extreme temperatures).
  * Central T-handle joystick controller (operable with pressurized spacesuit gloves).
  * High-Gain S-band umbrella parabolic dish antenna (0.9-meter diameter).
  * Ground-Controlled Television Assembly (GCTA) color camera steered remotely from Houston.
  * Dual 36-volt non-rechargeable silver-zinc potassium hydroxide batteries (121 Ah capacity each).
* **Major Scientific Discoveries & Achievements:**
  * Apollo 15: Enabled discovery of the 4.1-billion-year-old **"Genesis Rock"** (anorthosite sample 15415) at Spur Crater near Hadley Rille, proving the early Moon was covered by a global magma ocean.
  * Apollo 17: Enabled discovery of orange pyroclastic volcanic glass at Shorty Crater, proving explosive lunar volcanism 3.64 billion years ago.
  * Traversed 90.3 km (56.1 miles) across all three missions, hauling over 280 kg of lunar geological specimens.
* **Operational Status:**
  * Parked at designated VIP sites at conclusion of each mission.
  * **Status:** Inactive on lunar surface.

### 2.3 Viking 1 Lander
* **Official Registry:** NASA NSSDCA ID: `1975-075C` | Thomas A. Mutch Memorial Station
* **Launch Date:** August 20, 1975, 21:22:00 UTC (Titan IIIE-Centaur from Cape Canaveral LC-41).
* **Mars Landing Date:** July 20, 1976, 11:53:06 UTC (precisely 7 years after Apollo 11).
* **Surface Duration:** 2,245 Martian sols (6 Earth years, 116 days).
* **Historical Location:** Mars, Chryse Planitia (*Plains of Gold*), **22.48° N, 47.97° W**, elevation -2.69 km.
* **Hardware Location Type:** Permanent Martian Surface Preserved Artifact.
* **Original Purpose:** First successful U.S. soft-landing on Mars to characterize Martian geology, take high-resolution panoramic images, monitor atmospheric meteorology, and perform automated biological experiments searching for extraterrestrial life in Martian soil.
* **Key Equipment & Instruments:**
  * Dual SNAP-19 Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generators (RTGs) fueled by Plutonium-238 (~57 W power).
  * Gas Chromatograph Mass Spectrometer (GCMS) soil organic analyzer.
  * Biology Experiment Suite: Pyrolytic Release (PR), Labeled Release (LR), and Gas Exchange (GEX) chambers.
  * 3-meter retractable motorized surface sampler arm with backhoe collector scoop and sieve.
  * Meteorology boom (wind speed, wind direction, atmospheric pressure, temperature).
  * Dual facsimile scanning mirror cameras (360° color stereoscopic panoramas).
* **Major Scientific Discoveries & Achievements:**
  * Returned the first close-up color photographs from the Martian surface, revealing iron-oxide-rich red dust and a salmon-pink sky.
  * Characterized atmospheric composition: 95.3% CO₂, 2.7% N₂, 1.6% Ar, 0.13% O₂; surface pressure averaged 7.5 millibars.
  * Transmitted over 1,400 images and 2,245 sols of continuous meteorological records.
* **Operational Status:**
  * Final transmission received November 11, 1982, when a ground command accidentally overwrote antenna tracking angles.
  * **Status:** Inactive on Martian surface.

### 2.4 Sojourner Rover
* **Official Registry:** NASA NSSDCA ID: `1996-068B` | Microrover Flight Experiment (MFEX)
* **Mission:** Mars Pathfinder
* **Launch Date:** December 4, 1996, 06:58:07 UTC.
* **Mars Landing Date:** July 4, 1997, 16:56:55 UTC (delivered by Pathfinder).
* **Operating Duration:** **83 Martian sols (85 Earth days)** — Planned primary mission was only **7 Martian sols** (design goal: 30 sols).
* **Historical Location:** Mars, Ares Vallis (*Valley of Ares*), **19.33° N, 33.55° W**, elevation -3.68 km.
* **Hardware Location Type:** Permanent Martian Surface Preserved Artifact (~10 meters from lander).
* **Original Purpose:** Technology demonstration to test whether a small autonomous wheeled vehicle could survive, navigate, and perform in-situ contact science on Mars.
* **Dimensions & Mass:** 65 cm long, 48 cm wide, 30 cm tall; mass of **11.5 kg (25.4 lbs on Earth / 4.3 kg / 9.6 lbs on Mars)**.
* **Key Equipment & Instruments:**
  * Rocker-Bogie 6-wheel titanium suspension with 13-cm cleated aluminum wheels.
  * Alpha Particle X-Ray Spectrometer (APXS) using Curium-244 alpha radiation and X-ray emission.
  * Gallium Arsenide solar array deck (0.22 m², ~16 W peak power) and primary lithium thionyl chloride batteries.
  * 8-bit Intel 80C85 radiation-hardened CPU (2 MHz, 512 KB RAM).
  * Five forward laser diode hazard detection stripers and stereo CCD cameras.
* **Major Scientific Discoveries & Achievements:**
  * First mobile rover driven on Mars; traversed 104 meters around the landing site.
  * Conducted 16 APXS chemical analyses on rocks (e.g., "Barnacle Bill", "Yogi") and 9 soil tests; discovered high silica content (andesite), proving Mars experienced complex volcanic melting cycles.
  * Validated the Rocker-Bogie suspension architecture that became the standard for Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity, and Perseverance.
* **Operational Status:**
  * Final communication transmission received on September 27, 1997 (Sol 83).
  * **Status:** Inactive on Martian surface.

### 2.5 Mars Pathfinder Lander
* **Official Registry:** NASA NSSDCA ID: `1996-068A` | Carl Sagan Memorial Station
* **Mission:** Mars Pathfinder (Discovery Program Mission 2)
* **Launch Date:** December 4, 1996, 06:58:07 UTC.
* **Mars Landing Date:** July 4, 1997, 16:56:55 UTC (U.S. Independence Day).
* **Surface Duration:** 83 Martian sols (85 Earth days).
* **Historical Location:** Mars, Ares Vallis (*Valley of Ares*), **19.33° N, 33.55° W**, elevation -3.68 km.
* **Hardware Location Type:** Permanent Martian Surface Preserved Artifact.
* **Original Purpose:** Low-cost atmospheric entry, descent, and landing demonstration using a parachute, solid retrorockets, and a 24-lobe Kevlar airbag cluster; served as an imaging and meteorological base station and communications relay for the Sojourner rover.
* **Key Equipment & Instruments:**
  * 24-lobe Kevlar/Vectran omnidirectional impact airbag cluster (bounced 15 times up to 15 m high).
  * Tetrahedral self-righting solar petals driven by high-torque electric actuators.
  * Imager for Mars Pathfinder (IMP) stereoscopic mast camera with 12 color filters.
  * Atmospheric Structure Instrument / Meteorology Package (ASI/MET).
  * High-gain and low-gain telecommunications relay antennas for Sojourner microrover.
  * Deployable rover egress ramps.
* **Major Scientific Discoveries & Achievements:**
  * Transmitted 16,500 images; IMP stereo images revealed rounded river pebbles and imbricated (tilted/stacked) boulders aligned with ancient outflow currents, conclusively proving that catastrophic mega-floods hundreds of times larger than the Amazon River once carved Ares Vallis.
  * Validated the airbag landing technique subsequently used for Spirit and Opportunity in 2004.
* **Operational Status:**
  * Final data transmission received September 27, 1997. Mission officially concluded on March 10, 1998.
  * **Status:** Inactive on Martian surface.

### 2.6 Voyager 1 Spacecraft
* **Official Registry:** NASA NSSDCA ID: `1977-084A` | Voyager Interstellar Mission (VIM)
* **Launch Date:** September 5, 1977, 12:56:01 UTC (Titan IIIE-Centaur from Cape Canaveral LC-41).
* **Crucial Flybys:** Jupiter (March 5, 1979); Saturn (November 12, 1980).
* **Heliopause Crossing Date:** **August 25, 2012** (Distance: 121.6 AU / 18.2 billion km).
* **Current Distance (2024–2026):** **> 163 AU (> 24.4 billion km / > 15.2 billion miles)** from Earth. Speed: ~17 km/s (38,000 mph / 3.6 AU/year). One-way radio transit time: > 22.5 hours (> 45 hours round-trip).
* **Hardware Location Type:** **Travelling Through Interstellar Space (NOT on a planetary surface).**
* **Original Purpose:** Primary mission: Close-up exploration of the Jovian and Saturnian planetary systems. Extended mission: Traverse the solar termination shock and heliopause to measure interstellar space directly.
* **Key Equipment & Instruments:**
  * 3.7-meter High-Gain Antenna (HGA) dish transmitting ultra-faint 20-watt S/X-band signals.
  * Three Multihundred-Watt Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generators (MHW-RTGs) fueled by Plutonium-238.
  * *Active Science Instruments:*
    1. Magnetometer (MAG) on 13-meter boom (measures interstellar magnetic fields).
    2. Plasma Wave Subsystem (PWS) (measures interstellar electron density).
    3. Cosmic Ray Subsystem (CRS) (measures galactic cosmic rays).
    4. Low-Energy Charged Particle (LECP) instrument (measures interstellar ions).
  * The Voyager Golden Record: 12-inch gold-plated copper phonograph record carrying 115 images, 90 minutes of music, spoken greetings in 55 languages, and Earth sounds.
* **Major Scientific Discoveries & Achievements:**
  * Discovered active sulfur volcanism on Jupiter's moon Io; discovered Jupiter's ring system and two moons (Thebe and Metis); discovered complex ringlet structures at Saturn.
  * Captured the famous 1990 "Pale Blue Dot" solar system portrait from 6 billion km away.
  * First human-made spacecraft in history to enter interstellar space (August 2012), discovering a 40-fold surge in galactic plasma density beyond the heliopause.
* **Operational Status (2024–2026 Telemetry Audit):**
  * **Status: Active & Still Travelling.** In April–June 2024, NASA JPL engineers resolved an FDS memory chip issue from 24 billion km away, restoring science return from all 4 active instruments. RTGs will provide power until ~2028-2030, after which Voyager 1 will silently orbit the Milky Way galaxy.

## 3. Important Scientific Corrections Implemented

| Topic | Common Misconception | Verified Scientific Fact | Status in Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Apollo Lunar Rovers** | Often confused with Apollo 11. | Apollo 11 astronauts walked on foot; exactly three rovers were used on Apollo 15, 16, and 17. | Fully corrected across all datasets, stories, and quiz questions. |
| **Apollo 11 Hardware** | Often thought that the entire lander returned. | The descent stage served as a stationary launch pad and remains on the Moon; the ascent stage crashed after rendezvous. | Explicitly highlighted in all modules and timeline entries. |
| **Viking 1 Life Tests** | Often cited as proving or disproving life. | Experiments detected chemical reactivity caused by non-biological oxidants (perchlorates), but GCMS found zero organic carbon compounds. Inconclusive for life. | Accurately updated in missions, stories, timeline, and quiz. |
| **Pathfinder vs. Sojourner** | Sometimes treated as a single machine. | Pathfinder is the mothership lander station (Carl Sagan Station); Sojourner is the autonomous 6-wheeled microrover. | Distinct hardware IDs, cards, and stories maintained throughout. |
| **Sojourner Lifetime** | Often described without distinguishing design vs. actual life. | Planned primary mission: 7 Martian sols (30-sol design goal); actual operating lifetime: 83 Martian sols (85 Earth days). | Verified and highlighted in student Q&A, specifications, and quiz. |
| **Voyager 1 Location** | Often mistakenly classified as abandoned equipment. | Voyager 1 is NOT on a planet; it is actively travelling through interstellar space at ~38,000 mph. | Strictly distinguished in UI badges, museum simulator, and story logs. |
| **Voyager 1 Health** | Rumored to be dead after November 2023 binary glitch. | NASA JPL successfully patched FDS memory in mid-2024; all 4 working science instruments are returning telemetry. | Verified and updated with latest JPL press release citations. |

## 4. Uncertain & Conflicting Claims Documented

1. **Sojourner Mass:**
   * *Variance:* Some early press releases cited 10.6 kg (chassis dry mass without payload); official NASA JPL technical publications cite **11.5 kg (25.4 lbs)** including the APXS and deployment mechanism.
   * *Resolution:* Standardized to the official 11.5 kg total flight mass.
2. **Viking 1 Labeled Release Controversy:**
   * *Variance:* Experiment PI Gilbert Levin maintained that the Labeled Release gas surge indicated microbial metabolism; the broader NASA and scientific consensus concluded the reaction was inorganic chemical oxidation due to soil perchlorates and UV radiation.
   * *Resolution:* The application presents the established scientific consensus: chemical reactivity without organic carbon molecules, noting perchlorate oxidants.
3. **Voyager 1 Solar System Boundary:**
   * *Variance:* Some sources refer to crossing the heliopause as "leaving the solar system".
   * *Resolution:* Technically, the gravitational solar system extends to the outer edge of the Oort Cloud (~100,000 AU). Voyager 1 crossed the **Heliopause** (the boundary of the Sun's solar wind and magnetic field) into the interstellar medium at 121.6 AU, which is properly clarified in the text.
